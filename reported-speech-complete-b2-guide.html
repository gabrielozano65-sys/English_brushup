PART A · THE GRAMMAR

1What Is Reported Speech?

We use reported (or indirect) speech to tell someone what a person said, without repeating their exact words.

Direct speechAnna said, “I am tired and I want to go home.”
Reported speechAnna said (that) she was tired and she wanted to go home.

Four things can change

  1. The tense usually moves one step back in time (“backshift”): am → was.
  2. Pronouns and possessives change to fit the new speaker: I → she, my → her.
  3. Time and place words change: tomorrow → the next day, here → there.
  4. The structure changes for questions and commands: “Where do you live?” → She asked where I lived.

Say or tell?

VerbRuleExample
sayNo person after it (or say to + person)She said (that) she was late. · She said to me that she was late.
tellAlways followed by the personShe told me (that) she was late.
Remember that is optional after say and tell in everyday English: He said he was busy. = He said that he was busy.

DirectTom said to me, “I will call you tomorrow from my office here.”
ReportedTom told me he would call me the next day from his office there.
Careful If you report the words on the same day and in the same place, you don’t need to change the time and place words: “I’ll see you tomorrow,” she said this morning. → She said this morning she’d see me tomorrow.

4Reported Questions

The question becomes a statement: no inversion, no do/does/did, no question mark.

TypePatternExample
Wh- questionsasked + (obj) + wh-word + subject + verb“Where do you live?” → She asked me where I lived.
Yes/No questionsasked + (obj) + if / whether + subject + verb“Are you ready?” → He asked me if I was ready.
Questions with an auxiliaryThe auxiliary do/does/did disappears“What time does the film start?” → She wanted to know what time the film started.
Direct“Have you seen my keys?” he asked me.
ReportedHe asked me if I had seen his keys.
  • Useful alternatives to asked: wanted to know, wondered, enquired.
  • Use whether (not if) before or not or after a preposition: He asked whether or not I was coming.
  • A reported question is not a question, so we never write a question mark.

5Commands, Requests & Advice

We use a reporting verb + person + (not) to + infinitive. The tense does not change.

TypeDirectReported
Order“Close the window.”She told me to close the window.
Negative order“Don’t touch that!”He told us not to touch that.
Request“Could you open the door, please?”She asked me to open the door.
Advice“You should see a doctor.”He advised me to see a doctor.
Warning“Don’t go near the edge!”He warned us not to go near the edge.
Suggestion“Let’s go to the beach.”She suggested going to the beach. / She suggested (that) we go to the beach.
Watch out suggest is never followed by to + infinitive or by a person + to. Say She suggested going — not She suggested to go, and not She suggested me to go.

6Reporting Verbs

At B2 you are expected to go beyond said and told. Each verb has its own pattern — learn them together.

PatternVerbsExample
verb + that + clauseexplain, mention, complain, point out, claim, admit, announce, add, reply, insist, warn, promise, suggestHe explained that the train had been delayed.
verb + to-infinitiveoffer, promise, refuse, agree, threaten, demand, claimShe offered to pay for dinner.
They refused to leave.
verb + object + to-infinitiveadvise, ask, tell, order, remind, warn, invite, encourage, persuade, forbid, begHe reminded me to lock the door.
She advised us to wait.
verb + -ingadmit, deny, suggest, recommend, regretTom denied taking the money.
She suggested taking a taxi.
verb + (object) + preposition + -ingapologise for, accuse sb of, blame sb for, congratulate sb on, insist on, criticise sb for, thank sb forShe apologised for being late.
He accused me of lying.
They congratulated her on winning.
Why this matters for Cambridge In Part 4 the key word is very often a reporting verb (SUGGESTED, DENIED, ACCUSED, APOLOGISED…). If you know the pattern that follows it, half the question is already solved.

7When the Tense Does NOT Change

Backshift is common, but not compulsory in these cases.

SituationExample
The reporting verb is in the present (says, tells, has said)“I’m leaving tomorrow.” → He says he is leaving tomorrow.
The statement is a general truth or is still trueThe teacher said that the Earth goes (or went) round the Sun.
Modals with no past form: could, would, should, might, ought to“You should rest.” → The doctor said I should rest.
Past perfect (cannot go back further)“I had never seen it.” → She said she had never seen it.
Second and third conditionals“If I had more time, I would learn Italian.” → He said that if he had more time, he would learn Italian.
The facts are still true when you report them“The film starts at eight.” → She said the film starts (or started) at eight.

              PART C · CAMBRIDGE B2 FIRST — USE OF ENGLISH PART 4

              ★Key Word Transformations on Reported Speech

              In Part 4 you complete a second sentence so it means the same as the first, using the key word. Reported speech appears in almost every paper.

              Exam rules 2–5 words in the gap, including the key word · Never change the key word · Contractions count as two words (didn’t = did not) · Spelling must be correct · Each question is worth up to 2 marks (one for each correct part of the answer).

              A four-step method

              1. Identify the type of direct speech: statement, question, command, request, advice, promise…
              2. Use the key word to choose the structure. A reporting verb tells you the pattern (SUGGESTED → -ing, ADVISED → me to…). A question word or IF tells you it’s a reported question.
              3. Backshift and change pronouns and time words where necessary.
              4. Count your words and re-read the whole second sentence.
