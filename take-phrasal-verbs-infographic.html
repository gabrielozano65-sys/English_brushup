TAKE — Seven Essential Phrasal Verbs

Meanings, definitions and example sentences to master the most common phrasal verbs built on take.

take on take to take over take up take in take aside take off

Take on

  1. 1to accept a new responsibility, job or task
  2. 2to hire or employ someone
  3. 3to compete against an opponent

She decided to take on extra responsibilities at work.

The company is taking on ten new engineers this year.

Our team will take on the reigning champions next week.

Take to

  • to start liking someone or something quickly, or to begin a new habit almost naturally

He took to jogging every morning almost immediately.

She took to her new classmates straight away.

Take over

  • to gain control of something — a company, a country, a task — from someone else

The startup was taken over by a much larger competitor last year.

Take up

  1. 1to start a new hobby or activity
  2. 2to occupy space or time

She took up painting during the summer holidays.

That old sofa takes up far too much space.

Take in

  1. 1to understand or absorb information
  2. 2to deceive someone
  3. 3to give shelter to someone

It was hard to take in all the news at once.

I was completely taken in by his story.

The family took in two refugees during the crisis.

Take aside

  • to speak to someone privately, away from other people

The teacher took him aside to discuss his behaviour.

Take off

  1. 1for a plane to leave the ground
  2. 2to become suddenly successful or popular
  3. 3to remove clothing

The plane took off twenty minutes late.

Her career really took off after that award.

He took off his jacket as soon as he got home.
