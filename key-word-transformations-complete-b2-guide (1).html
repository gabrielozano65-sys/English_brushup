Part A — Strategy & Grammar Reference

Everything you need to know before you start practising.

1. What is Part 4?

Part 4 of the Use of English paper gives you 6 questions. Each question has:

  • An original sentence.
  • A key word in capital letters — you must not change it (no -s, -ed, -ing, un-, etc.).
  • A gapped sentence — you complete the gap so the second sentence means the same as the first.
Rules: Your answer must be 2–5 words including the key word. Contractions (e.g. don’t) count as two words. Spelling must be correct. Each question is worth 2 marks (total: 12). You can get 1 mark for a partially correct answer.
Timing: Spend about 10–12 minutes on Part 4 (out of 75 minutes for the whole paper). That gives you almost 2 minutes per question.

2. The 4-Step Method

#StepWhat to do
1READRead both sentences. Understand the meaning of the original.
2IDENTIFYLook at the key word. What grammar or vocabulary area does it signal? (See the decoder below.)
3BUILDWrite your answer: 2–5 words, including the key word, that complete the second sentence with the same meaning.
4CHECKSame meaning? 2–5 words? Key word unchanged? Spelling correct?

4. Passive Voice

Pattern: Subject + be (match the tense) + past participle (+ by + agent)
TenseActivePassive
Present simpleThey clean it.It is cleaned.
Past simpleThey cleaned it.It was cleaned.
Present perfectThey have cleaned it.It has been cleaned.
FutureThey will clean it.It will be cleaned.
Present continuousThey are cleaning it.It is being cleaned.
People say she is brilliant. → SAID → She is said to be brilliant.
Tip: With verbs like say, believe, think, know, expect, use: Subject + is/are said to + infinitive (or It is said that…).

5. Reported Speech

Part 4 usually tests reporting verbs + their grammatical pattern, not simple backshift. Learn these patterns:

PatternVerbsExample
verb + someone + to doadvise, remind, warn, encourage, invite, persuade, tell, ask, orderShe reminded me to lock the door.
verb + to dooffer, promise, agree, refuse, threatenHe offered to help.
verb + -ingsuggest, deny, admit, recommendHe denied stealing it.
verb + for + -ingapologise, blame, criticise, praise, thankShe apologised for being late.
verb + someone + of + -ingaccuse, suspect, convinceThey accused him of cheating.
verb + (that) + clausesuggest, insist, recommend, explainShe suggested that I join a gym.
Tip: The key word is always the reporting verb. If you know its pattern, the answer almost writes itself.

6. Conditionals & Wish

StructureUseFormula
2nd conditionalImaginary present/futureIf + past simple, would + infinitive
3rd conditionalImaginary pastIf + past perfect, would have + past participle
Wish + past simpleI want the present to be differentI wish I had more time.
Wish + past perfectI regret something in the pastI wish I had studied harder.
If onlySame as wish but strongerIf only you had come!
Had betterStrong advice (= should)You had better leave now.
I’m sorry I didn’t study harder. → WISH → I wish I had studied harder.
Tip: regret in the original sentence almost always signals wish + past perfect.

7. Comparisons

Core trick: reverse the comparison and adjust the adjective.
X is taller than Y ↔ Y is not as tall as X
X is the tallest ↔ Nobody is as tall as X / Nobody is taller than X
TransformationExample
comparative → not as…asShe is taller than me → I am not as tall as her.
superlative → comparativeIt’s the best → Nothing is better than it.
too ↔ enoughToo tired to go → Not energetic enough to go.
so…that ↔ such…thatIt was so cold that… ↔ It was such cold weather that…

I’m sure he didn’t do it on purpose. → HAVE → He can’t have done it on purpose.

9. Causative (have / get something done)

Pattern: Subject + have/get + object + past participle
Meaning: someone else does it for you.
A mechanic fixed my car. → HAD → I had my car fixed.
A mechanic is repairing my car now. → HAVING → I am having my car repaired now.
Tip: The subject changes from the doer to the person who arranged it.

10. Purpose & Result

StructureExample
in order to / so as toShe left early in order to catch the train.
in order not to / so as not toHe whispered so as not to wake the baby.
so that + clauseShe left early so that she could catch the train.

11. Connectors

Key equivalence:
although / even though / though + clause ↔ despite / in spite of + noun / -ing
unless = if…not
provided (that) / as long as = only if
Although it rained, we went out. → DESPITE → Despite the rain, we went out.
Common error: despite and in spite of are followed by a noun or -ing, never by a clause. Write “despite being tired”, NOT “despite she was tired”.

12. Vocabulary Patterns

Phrasal verbs — know the meaning and how to separate them:

Phrasal verbMeaningExample
give upstop doingHe gave up smoking.
put offpostponeWe put off the meeting.
call offcancelThey called off the match.
look forward toanticipate with pleasureI look forward to seeing you.
get overrecover fromIt took ages to get over the flu.
turn downrejectShe turned down the offer.

Fixed expressions:

ExpressionExample
it’s no use + -ingIt’s no use complaining.
it’s (not) worth + -ingIt is worth learning to play an instrument.
can’t wait (for)I can’t wait for the holidays.
succeed in + -ingShe succeeded in passing the test.
make up one’s mindI haven’t made up my mind.
have been + -ing + for/sinceHe has been playing guitar since he was ten.

13. Common Mistakes

#MistakeExample
1Changing the key wordKey word SUGGEST → writing “suggested” — WRONG. You must use the exact form given.
2Too many or too few wordsRemember: 2–5 words. Contractions = 2 words.
3Changing the meaningThe two sentences must mean exactly the same thing.
4Spelling errorsWatch irregular past participles: stolen, broken, chosen, written.
5Writing the whole sentenceOnly write the words that go in the gap, not the complete sentence.
6Not matching the tenseIf the original is past, your passive/modal must be past too.
