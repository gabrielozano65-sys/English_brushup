1Multiple-Choice Cloze

Vocabulary in context — collocations, phrasal verbs, and words that are easy to confuse.

Strategy

  • Read the whole text first for general meaning before choosing any answer.
  • Look at the words immediately before and after each gap — many answers depend on a fixed collocation or phrasal verb.
  • The four options are usually the same part of speech, so grammar alone won't eliminate them — focus on meaning and fixed use.
  • Watch prepositions that change meaning entirely: look at vs look after vs look for.

Commonly confused words

Word groupUse
say / tell / speak / talksay + words/that- (no person); tell + person; speak/talk about a topic
make / domake = create/produce (make a mistake, a decision); do = perform an activity (do homework, a favour)
look / see / watchlook = direct your eyes; see = perceive/notice; watch = observe something happening/moving
job / work / occupationjob = countable; work = uncountable; occupation = formal/official term
travel / trip / journeytravel = general activity (uncountable); trip = a single excursion; journey = the act of getting there
remember / remindremember = recall yourself; remind = make someone else recall

    2Open Cloze

    Grammar words only — the answer is never a noun, verb, adjective or adverb with independent meaning.

    What gets tested

    CategoryExamples
    Articlesa / an / the / (no article)
    Prepositionsin, on, at, by, since, with, about
    Relative pronounswho, which, that, whose, where
    Auxiliary verbsdo/does/did, have/has/had, am/is/are/was/were
    Modal verbscan, could, must, should, would, might
    Conjunctionsalthough, because, since, unless, while
    Quantifierssome, any, much, many, few, little, both, neither
    Other pronounsit, this, that, one, myself, each other

    Strategy

    • If your answer has clear standalone meaning (a noun, verb, adjective...), it's wrong — try again.
    • Look for fixed patterns: verb + preposition (depend on, look forward to) and multi-word linkers (as well as, in spite of).
    • Check subject–verb agreement and tense consistency around the gap.

      3Word Formation

      A base word is given in capitals — change its form (and often its word class) to fit the gap.

      Prefixes

      TypeExamples
      Negativeun- unhappy · in- inaccurate · im- impossible · il- illegal · ir- irregular · dis- disagree · non- nonsense
      Otherre- rewrite · over- overreact · under- underestimate · mis- misunderstand · pre- prepare

      Suffixes

      FormsExamples
      Nouns-tion/-sion decision · -ment employment · -ness happiness · -ity possibility · -er/-or teacher · -ance/-ence importance
      Adjectives-able/-ible comfortable · -ful careful · -less careless · -al national · -ive creative · -ous dangerous
      Adverbs-ly carefully
      Verbs-ise/-ize modernise · -en strengthen · -ify simplify
      • Decide what part of speech the gap needs (noun / verb / adjective / adverb) from the sentence structure before you pick a prefix or suffix.
      • Check whether a negative prefix is also needed — it's easy to build the right word but forget it should mean the opposite.

        4Key Word Transformation

        Rewrite a sentence using a given word, keeping the exact same meaning, in 2–5 words.

        Structures worth knowing cold

        Passive voice object → subject + be + past participle They built the house in 1990. → The house was built in 1990.
        Reported speech tense shifts back one step; pronouns/time expressions change "I'll call you," she said. → She said she would call me.
        Conditionals 0 (general truth) · 1 (real future) · 2 (unreal present) · 3 (unreal past) · mixed I didn't study, so I failed. → If I had studied, I wouldn't have failed.
        Comparatives & as...as see the dedicated Comparisons guide for full detail Tom is more experienced than Sam. → Sam isn't as experienced as Tom.
        Modals of deduction must have / can't have / might have + past participle I'm sure he didn't know. → He can't have known.
        Wish / if only wish + past simple (present regret) · wish + past perfect (past regret) I regret not studying harder. → I wish I had studied harder.
        Too / enough too + adjective + to-infinitive · adjective + enough + to-infinitive This box is too heavy for me to lift. → This box isn't light enough for me to lift.
        So / such...that so + adjective/adverb + that · such (a/an) + adjective + noun + that It was such a good film that we watched it twice.
